Skruxem – Privacy Policy

Effective: 11 August 2026  ·  Last updated: 11 August 2026

Skruxem is a document annotation and signing app for Android, developed by SkruxemDev. This policy explains what data the app accesses and what it does with it. It covers the Skruxem Android app only.

For the purposes of applicable data protection and privacy law, the entity responsible for the processing described in this policy — referred to as the data controller, data fiduciary, or equivalent depending on the jurisdiction — is SkruxemDev, reachable at the address in Section 23. It is worth being concrete about what that responsibility actually covers in practice, because it is narrower than the term "data controller" often implies. The only data SkruxemDev itself ever holds is email correspondence received at its contact address, which is a Gmail account. There are no servers, databases, or data pipelines operated by SkruxemDev beyond that inbox. When someone sends an email to SkruxemDev, the message arrives in that Gmail account, is read and replied to by one person, and that is the full extent of what SkruxemDev holds. All other processing described in this policy — opening and annotating documents, storing drafts, saving signatures — takes place entirely on your own device and never passes through any system SkruxemDev operates.

Whenever no prior acceptance is recorded on your device — on first install, after clearing the app's data, or after uninstalling and reinstalling — a non-dismissible acceptance screen is displayed before the app can be used. That screen sets out important context about Skruxem's nature and its limitations as a document annotation and signing tool, including that Skruxem does not certify or attest to the legal validity of any signature placed through it, and that users are responsible for confirming whether Skruxem's output is suitable for any specific legal or contractual purpose. The screen includes a checkbox which must be ticked to confirm that you have read and agree to the Terms of Use and this Privacy Policy; the "I understand" button becomes available only once that checkbox is ticked. Acceptance is recorded locally via a small flag stored on your device (described in Sections 12 and 15); that flag contains no personal information. It is intentionally excluded from Android Auto Backup so that the acceptance screen is always displayed when the app is first used on a new or reset device. The screen does not result in any data being sent to SkruxemDev.

This policy does not extend to third-party apps, services, or destinations that you may interact with through or because of using Skruxem — for example, an app on your device that converts a file format, a cloud storage service you choose to save a document to, or any app you share a document with via Android's share sheet. Each of those services is governed by its own privacy policy. Where a specific third-party service is directly relevant to how Skruxem operates, it is identified and described in the sections below.

This policy document is hosted on GitHub Pages (hostingtempsavail-beep.github.io/Skruxem-sites/privacy_policy.html). When you load this page, GitHub's infrastructure may incidentally process standard web server data — such as your IP address, browser type, and the time of the request — as a byproduct of delivering the page. SkruxemDev does not control, receive, or have access to that data. GitHub's handling of this data is governed by GitHub's privacy statement. SkruxemDev does not install any analytics, tracking scripts, or cookies on this policy page; there is no SkruxemDev-operated server code behind this page, so browser Do Not Track (DNT) signals have no applicable infrastructure to act on here.

This policy is written in English. No other language versions are currently available. If you have a question about its content and English is not your first language, you are welcome to reach out at the address in Section 23 and we will do our best to assist.

1. Data SkruxemDev Does Not Collect

Skruxem itself does not collect, transmit, store, or share any personal information. Specifically:

  • No account or registration is required.
  • Skruxem itself includes no analytics, crash reporting, or tracking SDKs. (The Android platform may independently collect aggregated crash and performance data through Android Vitals for users who have opted in to share usage and diagnostics with Google — this is carried out by Google independently of Skruxem and is described in Section 9.)
  • No advertisements are displayed, and no user data is used for advertising purposes.
  • No document content is ever uploaded to any server by SkruxemDev or by Skruxem itself. Where a file is stored in a cloud service such as Google Drive and requires conversion before it can be displayed, the cloud provider handles that conversion within its own infrastructure — Skruxem requests a rendered output via Android's Storage Access Framework and does not transmit file content to any server. See Section 2 for detail.
  • No usage data, device identifiers, or location data are transmitted off your device, shared with any party, or collected externally. Any counters or flags the app stores locally (such as a record of how many times an in-app tip has been shown) exist solely on your device and are not personal information.
  • No personal information is sold, rented, or shared for commercial purposes.
  • No marketing, promotional, or commercial electronic communications are sent to any user or to any individual who contacts SkruxemDev. Skruxem has no newsletter, no email marketing program, and no promotional outreach of any kind.

Skruxem is designed to collect and retain only what is strictly necessary for the app to function. This data minimization principle is reflected throughout the practices described in this policy. The legal basis for the limited processing described here is set out in the applicable supplement in Section 19; EU and EEA users will find a full Article 13 GDPR lawful basis table there, and the same analysis informs the equivalent disclosures in the UK, Brazil, and India supplements.

Tracking signals and opt-out mechanisms. Skruxem does not use cookies, advertising trackers, or any browser-based or app-based tracking technology. As a result, browser-initiated Do Not Track (DNT) signals and Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals have no applicable infrastructure to act on in connection with Skruxem's operation — there is nothing to opt out of. Several U.S. state privacy laws (including California, Colorado, Virginia, Oregon, New Jersey, and others) require covered businesses to honor universal opt-out signals; because Skruxem neither collects personal data for targeted advertising nor sells personal data, these requirements do not impose obligations on SkruxemDev, and Skruxem's design is consistent with the intent those mechanisms serve.

One limited exception — contact and inquiry correspondence. The above describes what the app itself collects. There is one circumstance in which SkruxemDev as a company may hold personal information: if anyone contacts us by email for any reason — whether that is a question about the app, a support issue, a privacy rights request, general feedback, a press or business inquiry, or anything else. In that case, we receive and temporarily hold the sender's email address and the content of their message, solely to respond to the inquiry. This applies regardless of whether the sender is a Skruxem user. That information is not used for any other purpose, is not shared with third parties except as required by law, and is retained only as long as reasonably needed to handle the matter and for no longer than 90 days after it is closed, unless a longer period is required by applicable law or an ongoing legal matter. This is the only context in which SkruxemDev itself may hold personal information about any individual. See Section 23 for the contact address.

No combining of data across sources. SkruxemDev does not combine personal information from different sources. The only personal information attributable to SkruxemDev originates from one of two sources: the on-device operation of the app, or direct contact with us by email. These two categories are handled separately and are not cross-referenced with each other or with any external data source. No personal information about you is obtained from any third party, data broker, advertising network, or partner.

2. Files and Documents

Skruxem can open PDF files and images (such as JPG and PNG). Documents you open are read from your device's local storage using Android's Storage Access Framework. All processing happens entirely on-device. Skruxem does not collect, copy, or store the files you open — they are read in place from wherever they already live on your device, processed in memory for display, and not retained by the app. “Files and docs” appears in Skruxem’s Google Play Data Safety declaration solely to disclose this on-device read access, not to indicate that any file content is collected or transmitted.

For file types that Skruxem cannot render natively when opening them in the editor — including Office formats such as Word, Excel, and PowerPoint; OpenDocument formats (ODT, ODS, ODP); TXT; HTML; RTF; and EPUB — the app requests a PDF rendition from the content provider behind the URI using Android's Storage Access Framework. For example, if the file is stored in Google Drive, Skruxem asks the Google Drive app already on your device to return a PDF version of it; the Drive app handles any server-side conversion internally, within Google's own infrastructure, and delivers the result back to Skruxem. Skruxem does not send your file content to any external server — it only receives the rendered output. The provider already holds the file; Skruxem is requesting a different format of something the provider was already storing. Skruxem does not control what the provider does on its end during that process; its own privacy policy governs it. It is your responsibility to review the privacy policy of any such provider before opening sensitive documents through it. If no capable provider is available, the file is rejected and you are returned to the home screen.

Where a conversion to PDF succeeds, the resulting file is stored temporarily in the app's private cache directory for the duration of your editing session and is deleted automatically when you close the editor. It is not accessible to other apps.

Convert to PDF feature. Skruxem also includes a standalone Convert to PDF feature, accessible from the home screen via the Convert button. This feature converts files to PDF entirely within Skruxem itself — no other app on your device is involved. It supports two input types: JPEG and PNG images (converted using Android's built-in PDF rendering APIs) and DOCX Word documents (converted using the Apache POI library bundled with the app, with a fallback XML parser for devices where the library cannot initialise). All conversion processing happens on your device. The resulting PDF is stored temporarily in the app's private cache directory and is deleted when you dismiss the result screen or when the app session ends. If the app is interrupted — for example, by a low-memory shutdown — the path to the temporary file is preserved across the interruption so that the result can be displayed when you return; the file itself remains in the cache directory until that session is resolved or the cache is cleared. You can then save the converted PDF to a location of your choice via the system file picker, share it via the Android share sheet, or open it directly in the editor for annotation. Skruxem does not retain the file after you have saved or shared it. No document content leaves your device through this feature.

When you save an annotated document, Skruxem writes a temporary export file to app-specific external storage (Android/data/com.skruxem.app/files/exports/). On devices where external storage is unavailable, the file is written to the app's private internal cache directory instead. Either way, it is immediately handed to the Android system — either to a destination you choose via the system file picker (Downloads, a connected cloud service, or any other location your device offers) or to another app via the Android share sheet. The staging file is removed from the exports directory at the start of the next save operation; if the editor is closed before another save is made, the file is released from protection at that point and cleaned up by the next save from any document. Skruxem does not decide where the final file is stored; that choice belongs entirely to you through the system dialog.

No document content ever leaves your device through Skruxem itself.

Password-protected documents. Skruxem does not support opening password-protected or encrypted PDF files. Android's PDF rendering engine rejects encrypted files at the point of opening, before any content is displayed. When this happens, the document is silently rejected and you are returned to the home screen with a notification that the file could not be opened. No password is ever requested, entered, stored, or handled by Skruxem. If you need to annotate a password-protected PDF, you would need to remove the password using another tool before opening it in Skruxem.

Metadata in exported documents. When Skruxem exports an annotated PDF, the output file is assembled as a fresh document rather than a modified copy of the original. As a result, document-level metadata fields — such as Author, Title, Subject, and Keywords — are not carried forward from the source and are not set to any value. The only metadata field populated in the exported file is the Producer field, which is set automatically by the PDFBox library to a library identifier string containing no personal information. A creation date and modification date reflecting the time of export may also be set by the library. No device identifiers, account information, or any other personally identifying data are added to the exported file's metadata. For unannotated pages imported verbatim from a source PDF, the page content itself is preserved as-is, but the document-level metadata fields described above are not inherited.

3. Annotations

Skruxem supports three types of annotations: highlights (which may optionally have a comment attached), text overlays, and signatures placed on a document. All annotation data is held in memory during your editing session and on your device only. When you save, annotations are flattened permanently into the exported PDF on your device — they are not stored separately or transmitted anywhere.

Comment text is included in the comments panel view within the app but is not embedded as metadata in saved PDF files; only the visual highlight is burned into the PDF.

Signature images in exported documents. When you place a signature annotation on a document and export it, the signature image is permanently embedded into the resulting PDF. Once you share that exported file with a third party — by emailing it, uploading it to a cloud service, or delivering it to a counterparty — the embedded signature image is part of that file and is handled entirely by whoever receives it, under their own practices and policies. Skruxem does not retain the exported file after handing it off, as described in Section 2, and has no visibility into or control over what happens to the file — or the signature image within it — once it reaches its destination. If you are using Skruxem to sign documents in a professional or contractual context, it is worth being aware that the file you share is the complete record of your signature on that document, and its handling after delivery is outside Skruxem's scope.

Electronic signature validity. Whether a signature placed through Skruxem satisfies the requirements for a legally recognised electronic signature depends on the law applicable in your jurisdiction, the specific requirements of the document or transaction involved, and the acceptance of the counterparty or any authority receiving the signed document. Skruxem does not certify, attest to, or guarantee that any signature produced through the app meets the standards required by any particular electronic signature framework — including the Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN) and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) in the United States, Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 (eIDAS), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (eIDAS 2.0), in the European Union, the Electronic Communications Act 2000 and associated regulations in the United Kingdom, the Electronic Transactions Act No. 19 of 2006 in Sri Lanka, the Electronic Transactions Act 1999 (Cth) and equivalent state and territory legislation in Australia, the Information Technology Act, 2000 (as amended) in India, Law No. 14,063 of 2020 (Lei de Assinaturas Eletrônicas) in Brazil, provincial Electronic Transactions Acts in Canada (most of which are based on the Uniform Electronic Commerce Act, including Ontario's Electronic Commerce Act, 2000, British Columbia's Electronic Transactions Act, and Alberta's Electronic Transactions Act), or any equivalent legislation elsewhere. If you are signing documents that have specific legal requirements, you should seek appropriate advice to confirm whether Skruxem's output is suitable for that purpose. This consideration is also addressed in the acceptance screen displayed on first launch.

4. Signature Library

You can draw and save signatures to a personal signature library for reuse across documents. Saved signatures are stored as PNG image files in the app's private internal storage (/data/data/com.skruxem.app/files/signatures/, or /data/user/0/com.skruxem.app/files/signatures/ on multi-user Android setups — this directory is not visible in file managers and is accessible only to the app itself). Up to 30 signatures are retained; the oldest are pruned automatically when this limit is reached. When you delete a signature from within the app, its PNG file is removed immediately and permanently from that private directory. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data (Settings → Apps → Skruxem → Storage → Clear Data) also deletes all files in that directory.

Signature library files are included in Android's Auto Backup to your Google account, so they can be restored if you switch or reset your device. Google's own privacy policy governs that cloud storage. If you do not want your signatures backed up to the cloud, you can disable Auto Backup for Skruxem in your device's Google account backup settings. Signature library files are held only in local device storage, not account-linked: they live in the app's private internal storage on your device and are not directly tied to or queryable from your Google account. Auto Backup is the only automatic mechanism by which they can transfer to a new or reset device, and only if Auto Backup was enabled and a successful backup completed before the device change or reset; if backup was not enabled or did not complete successfully, the library cannot be recovered automatically.

Saved signatures are also included in Android's device-to-device transfer when you set up a new Android device (for example, via a cable transfer or Nearby Share during device setup). This mechanism does not involve cloud storage; data moves directly between devices. You can manage or disable device-to-device transfer in your Android device settings during the setup process.

Signatures placed on a document as annotations are stored only as part of a draft (see Section 5) and are not part of the signature library.

A number of U.S. states have enacted laws that regulate biometric data. The most relevant to a signature feature are Illinois, Texas, Colorado, and Washington. Each is addressed below. In all cases, the threshold question is whether SkruxemDev "collects" or "processes" signature data in the legal sense — and the answer is no: signature images are created and stored entirely on your own device, and SkruxemDev never receives them on any server it operates. Where signature images are included in Android Auto Backup, they are carried by Android's system-level backup service to your own Google account — not to SkruxemDev. Google's handling of that data is governed by Google's privacy policy. If you prefer that signature images not leave your device at all, you can disable Auto Backup for Skruxem as described above.

Illinois — Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA, 740 ILCS 14): BIPA defines "biometric identifier" as a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry. The statute explicitly excludes "writing samples, written signatures" from that definition. A 2024 amendment (P.A. 103-0769) separately added "electronic signature" to BIPA's definition of "written release" — the legally sufficient form of consent under the Act — and capped per-person damages for repeated scans. That amendment addressed the consent mechanism and damages framework; it did not alter the definitional exclusions for biometric identifiers. The original written signature exclusion already covers handwritten signatures drawn in Skruxem. BIPA does not apply to this feature.

Texas — Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 503): CUBI defines biometric identifiers as a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or record of hand or face geometry. Handwritten signatures are not listed. CUBI also applies only to identifiers captured for a commercial purpose by the entity that captures them — SkruxemDev does not capture or receive signature data at all. CUBI does not apply to this feature. (Note: CUBI was amended in 2025 by the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (TRAIGA) in ways that affect AI-related biometric use; those amendments have no bearing on Skruxem's local signature storage.)

Colorado — Biometric provisions of the Colorado Privacy Act (CPA, HB 24-1130, effective July 1, 2025): Colorado's biometric amendments to the CPA use a broader definition of "biometric identifier" than Illinois or Texas, and do not enumerate the same explicit signature exclusion. However, the CPA's obligations apply to entities that act as "controllers" or "processors" of biometric data — meaning entities that determine the purposes and means of processing, or that process data on a controller's behalf. Because SkruxemDev never receives, stores, or processes signature data on any system it operates, it does not function as a controller or processor of that data under the CPA. The CPA does not impose obligations on SkruxemDev in connection with this feature.

Washington — Biometric privacy provisions (RCW 19.375): Washington's commercial biometric privacy statute defines "biometric identifier" as data generated by automatic measurements of an individual's biological characteristics, listing fingerprints, voiceprints, eye retinas, and irises, with a catch-all covering other unique biological patterns or characteristics used to identify a specific individual. Unlike Illinois's BIPA, Washington's statute does not specifically enumerate hand geometry or face geometry scans. Handwritten signatures are not listed or covered by the catch-all. As with the other frameworks above, SkruxemDev's non-collection position means no obligations arise regardless of classification.

Oregon — Oregon Consumer Data Privacy Act (OCPA, SB 619, effective July 1, 2024): Oregon's OCPA includes biometric data as a category of sensitive personal data. The definition covers personal data generated by automatic measurements of biological characteristics that allow or confirm the unique identification of a consumer. Unlike BIPA, the OCPA does not include an explicit written-signature exclusion. Even so, the threshold question remains whether SkruxemDev collects or processes that data — and it does not. Signature images are created and stored on your own device; SkruxemDev never receives them on any server it operates. The OCPA does not impose obligations on SkruxemDev in connection with this feature.

Maryland — Maryland Online Data Privacy Act (MODPA, effective October 2025): Maryland's MODPA classifies biometric data as sensitive personal data. As with Oregon, the MODPA's obligations apply to controllers and processors of personal data — entities that determine the purposes and means of processing, or that process data on a controller's behalf. Because SkruxemDev never receives, stores, or processes signature data on any system it operates, it does not function as a controller or processor of that data under the MODPA.

California — CCPA/CPRA sensitive personal information: Under the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CCPA/CPRA), biometric information is classified as sensitive personal information, subject to heightened protections including restrictions on use and a right to limit that use. The same non-collection logic that applies under the frameworks above applies equally to California: because SkruxemDev never receives signature data on any server it operates, it does not collect or process biometric information as defined by the CCPA/CPRA, and the heightened protections for sensitive personal information do not impose obligations on SkruxemDev in connection with this feature.

5. Drafts

Skruxem automatically saves your in-progress work as a draft approximately two seconds after each annotation change, and saves immediately when you leave the editor. If you leave without explicitly saving, your work is already preserved. Drafts are stored as JSON files in private internal storage (/data/data/com.skruxem.app/files/drafts/, or /data/user/0/com.skruxem.app/files/drafts/ on multi-user Android setups — this directory is not visible in file managers and is accessible only to the app itself). Up to 50 drafts are retained. When you delete a draft from within the app, its JSON file is removed immediately and permanently from that private directory. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data (Settings → Apps → Skruxem → Storage → Clear Data) also deletes all files in that directory.

Each draft JSON file contains the following: a schema version number; a URI reference pointing to the original document's location on your device (not the document content itself); the document's filename; a page count; a sequential comment counter used to number annotations; a last-edited timestamp; and the annotation data for each page. Annotation data consists of position coordinates and type-specific fields for each annotation you placed — specifically: text content, font size, colour, and maximum line width for text overlays; position, dimensions, colour, a stable annotation identifier, and — when a comment is attached — the comment text and its sequential comment number for highlights; and, for signature annotations placed directly in the document (as distinct from signatures saved in the signature library described in Section 4), the bitmap of the signature stored as a Base64-encoded PNG image within the JSON file. Draft files do not contain document page content, page thumbnails, or any rendering of the underlying document — only the annotation data described above and the URI reference to the original file's location on your device.

Drafts are held only in local device storage, not account-linked, no backup, and are not included in Android's Auto Backup or device-to-device transfer. They reference the original document's location on your device and are intentionally excluded because those location references are device-specific and would not be valid after a restore or on a new device. Unlike the signature library and supporter status (Section 6), which are backed up and can be restored on a new device via Android's backup service if backup was enabled and completed successfully before the device change, drafts have no automatic cross-device transfer path at all — they exist only on the device where they were created.

6. Android Backup and Device Transfer

Android provides two mechanisms that may move a small set of app data off your current device: Auto Backup (cloud backup to your Google account) and device-to-device transfer (direct transfer when setting up a new device, governed by dataExtractionRules on Android 12 and above). Skruxem configures both mechanisms identically — the same data is included or excluded in each.

The data included in both Auto Backup and device transfer is limited to:

  • Saved signature library — PNG files you have drawn and saved (/data/data/com.skruxem.app/files/signatures/).
  • Theme preference — your chosen Light / System / Dark setting.
  • Supporter status — two flags stored together in a single preferences file: a permanent flag recording that a support purchase was completed (so recognised supporter status is not lost on a new device), and a flag recording whether a Developer Club subscription was active at the time of the last backup (updated on each billing check; the billing layer corrects any stale value the next time the in-app support options are opened after restore). Both flags, and the signature library above, are held only in local device storage, not account-linked: they are held in the app's private local storage and cannot be recovered by querying your Google account. Unlike the Developer Club subscription check (Section 7) — which queries Google Play and resolves against whichever Google account is signed in — these items have no account-side representation. Backup is therefore the only automatic restoration path for all three — and only if Auto Backup was enabled and completed successfully before the device change or reinstall.

The following are explicitly excluded from both Auto Backup and device transfer:

  • Documents (Skruxem never stores the documents you open).
  • Drafts (device-specific file references; see Section 5).
  • The first-launch acceptance flag (intentionally excluded so the acceptance screen is always shown on a new device).

Google's privacy policy governs the storage and handling of Auto Backup data. You can disable or manage cloud backup in your device's Google account settings, and device-to-device transfer in your Android device settings during device setup.

7. In-App Support Purchases (Google Play Billing)

Skruxem offers optional support purchases — one-time Supporter Tokens and monthly Developer Club subscriptions — processed entirely by Google Play Billing. Skruxem never sees or stores your payment details. When the in-app support screen is opened, Skruxem asks Google Play whether your account holds an active subscription or a prior Supporter Token purchase; Google Play returns only the status of its own in-app products for your account (token identifiers and purchase state). This status flows directly from Google Play to the app on your device — it is not stored by Skruxem beyond the current session and is not transmitted to SkruxemDev’s servers. This billing check is what Google Play’s Data Safety form refers to as “purchase history” — it is a status query, not a collection or retention of your purchase records by SkruxemDev. The same applies to the outcome of any new transaction you complete. Google's privacy policy governs any data collected during that transaction. These purchases are entirely voluntary. Making a purchase unlocks a personal thank-you screen inside the app and removes a one-time-per-session save prompt (non-supporters reach the same save options after the prompt; it does not block any functionality). Beyond these, no purchase unlocks additional features or removes restrictions — all core app functionality is available without purchasing anything.

A Supporter Token is a single, non-recurring payment — "one-time" describes the billing structure, not a restriction on frequency. There is no limit to how many Supporter Tokens you can purchase; each is an independent transaction. A Supporter Token does not unlock features, remove restrictions, or provide a content entitlement — it is a way to contribute financially to the project, and the in-app perk is a personal thank-you screen that acknowledges your support.

If a purchase cannot be fully acknowledged or consumed during your session — for example because connectivity drops immediately after a transaction — Skruxem schedules a short-lived background task via Android WorkManager to retry the billing acknowledgement (for subscriptions) or consumption (for one-time tokens) once connectivity is restored, typically around one hour after the original purchase. That background connection reaches only Google Play Billing; no server operated by Skruxem or SkruxemDev is involved.

The Developer Club is a recurring subscription. It renews automatically on a monthly basis and continues until you cancel. To cancel, open the Google Play app, tap your profile icon, go to Payments & subscriptions  ›  Subscriptions, select Developer Club, and tap Cancel subscription. Google Play's own cancellation and refund terms apply.

Account-linked status. Developer Club subscriptions are linked to your Google account, not your device. To check subscription status, Skruxem uses Android's Billing Library, which communicates with the Google Play Store app installed on your device. The Play Store app retrieves the subscription records held by the Google account currently signed into Google Play and returns the result to Skruxem. No request passes through SkruxemDev's servers — the query travels directly from the Billing Library to the Play Store, and from there to Google's servers. Because the check is account-based, your subscription is recognised on any Android device where the same Google account is signed into Google Play. This check runs every time the in-app support options are opened, meaning the status always reflects the current state. The four possible outcomes of that check are:

  • Active subscription on the currently signed-in account — Google Play returns the active subscription; the monthly subscription purchase options within the support screen are shown as unavailable for selection.
  • Subscription cancelled or expired on the currently signed-in account — Google Play returns no active subscription; the monthly subscription purchase options become available again automatically.
  • Never subscribed on the currently signed-in account — Google Play finds nothing; the monthly subscription purchase options are available.
  • Different Google account signed into Google Play — the check reads whichever account is signed into Google Play on the device at the moment the support options are opened, not any account used previously. If that account holds an active Developer Club subscription the monthly options are unavailable; if it does not, they are available — regardless of any subscriptions held by other accounts on other devices.

How the billing check relates to your thank-you screen. The billing check described above governs only how the support screen's purchase options are displayed — it does not control whether your thank-you screen is visible. That is governed by a separate local flag stored on your device. Unlike the subscription check above, which is account-based and resolves on any device signed into the same Google account, this flag is held only in local device storage, not account-linked: it exists only in the app's private local storage on the device where the purchase was confirmed, and cannot be recovered by any account query. Once written at the point of purchase confirmation, this flag is not cleared by any billing event, including subscription cancellation or expiry. If you cancel a subscription while the app remains installed, the billing check will return the "subscription cancelled or expired" outcome listed above — making the monthly purchase options available again — but your thank-you screen will continue to be accessible, because the local flag on your device was already written before the cancellation and is unaffected by it. The flag is removed only if you uninstall the app, clear its data, or reset your device.
