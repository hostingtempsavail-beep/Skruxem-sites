About Skruxem

Skruxem is provided by SkruxemDev — a trade name used by the individual developer who created and maintains the app. Throughout these Terms, "we", "us", and "our" refer to SkruxemDev. Skruxem is an Android application that lets you open documents and image files stored on your device, place hand-drawn or typed signatures on them, add text annotations, highlights, and comments, and save or share the resulting file — with all annotation and document processing handled on your own device. The app also includes a Convert to PDF feature, accessible from the home screen, that converts image files (JPEG and PNG) and Word documents (DOCX) into PDF entirely on your device, without sending anything to a server. The converted PDF can be saved to a location of your choosing or opened directly in the editor to annotate. The app is currently free to download and use and contains no advertising — both of which may change in a future update.

Skruxem operates without accounts, without cloud storage, and without transmitting your documents to any server. Every annotation you place stays on your device, and the app itself never sends your document content anywhere. The one nuance worth knowing upfront: non-PDF file support falls into two groups. Image files — JPEG and PNG — open directly, with no conversion step. Document formats such as Word documents, spreadsheets, and presentations are converted to a displayable format before Skruxem can render them. For document files stored locally on your device, the app requests this conversion through the content provider — the software component Android uses to deliver the file to the app, which can optionally convert it to a different format in the process. Whether this succeeds depends on whether that provider supports PDF conversion; most local device-storage providers do not, so locally stored document files may instead show an unsupported-format message. If the file is stored in a cloud service like Google Drive, the cloud provider's own systems may instead perform this conversion step on their servers before delivering the file to the app — cloud storage providers are more likely to support it, but this is not guaranteed for every file type or service. That server-side conversion is handled entirely by the cloud provider, not by Skruxem or SkruxemDev, and is described in more detail in § 20.

The app accesses only files you explicitly open through your device's file picker. It does not browse, index, or access any other part of your device's storage.

A small amount of app data may be backed up automatically via Android's built-in backup service to your Google account. A few categories of data are deliberately excluded — notably your source documents (which the app never copies or stores) and your drafts (which contain device-specific file references that become invalid after a restore on a different device or factory reset). § 5 has the complete picture of what is and isn't included, and why.

The app makes active internet connections for three specific, limited purposes: to process optional in-app purchases through Google Play; to check for and receive app updates; and to check whether these Terms or the Privacy Notice have been updated. That last check fetches a small JSON file from the app's legal version endpoint (hosted on GitHub Pages) on each fresh launch — it carries no document content and no personal data, and is used solely to detect whether either legal document has changed since you last acknowledged it. If a change is detected, the app shows the update notice described in § 16. The app also reads your device's network state through a passive, read-only system permission — one that makes no active connection of its own — so it can show you a helpful message when Google Play is temporarily unreachable. None of these involves transmitting the content of your documents in any way. If an update is classified as high-priority, the update check triggers a mandatory installation flow that must be completed before the app can continue to be used — see § 20 for detail.

The app declares three Android permissions:

  • INTERNET — required for billing and in-app update checks;
  • com.android.vending.BILLING — required by Google Play to process in-app purchases;
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — used to check whether Google Play is reachable before showing connectivity-related messages.

The app does not request any file-storage permission at the operating system level — files are accessed exclusively through Android's Storage Access Framework, which means every file the app can see is one you've explicitly opened through Android's file picker — which includes both local device storage and connected cloud services such as Google Drive, Dropbox, and OneDrive. The app does not currently request a camera permission.

Skruxem currently requires Android 8.0 (Oreo, API level 26) or later; this minimum may be raised in a future update, and if so that will be noted in the update's release notes. The app is designed to work well on current Android releases, though some features — particularly the ability to view and annotate non-PDF file types such as Office documents — depend on capabilities provided by the Android platform and your specific device, and may behave differently across manufacturers, device models, and Android versions. That aspect is outside SkruxemDev's control.

Skruxem is designed to work with Android's accessibility services. The document editor includes a virtual view hierarchy that supports TalkBack, Android's built-in screen reader, allowing document content, annotations, and controls to be navigated by touch and voice. The app's layouts also support right-to-left mirroring for devices set to a right-to-left language.
